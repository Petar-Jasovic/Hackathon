Welcome to the landmarks of Budva

Want to know in-depth about town of Budva? Why not start with some of the towns most famous landmarks!

Unlock exclusive content by today!

  • Citadel

    • Budva is surrounded by walls, and the city fortress-citadel is located on the southern part of the ramparts. In the past, this place served as a shelter for the locals and is the highest part of the Budva ramparts. Legend has it that a couple in love, whose parents defended their love, jumped from the citadel into the sea and both turned into fish. After all, it is only a legend, but today two fish are the symbol of the city, which can be seen carved into the stone of the citadel, above the entrance. On the fortress there are the remains of the foundation of the church of St. Mary, after which the fortress got its name. Locals call it Saint Mary, and for tourists it is the Citadel. The fortress has a city library (an attraction that should definitely not be missed) and a restaurant, from whose terrace you can enjoy the view of the marina and the city. With a good book that can be rented, wine or coffee, with a view of the sea, sometimes is all a person needs. The entrance to the Citadel is located next to the Church Square. The Citadel is connected to the city walls that surround the city and are about 1 kilometer long. .According to old drawings, it is known that Budva had as many as 6 towers that surrounded the city. After the earthquake of 1667, the towers were destroyed, and the ramparts remained, on which it is possible to walk and see the city from a slightly different perspective. Through the entrance to the citadel, you can also reach the city ramparts.

  • Museum of the city of Budva

    • The museum in the Old Town, located in a building from the beginning of the 19th century, has permanent exhibitions of archaeological and ethnographic collections, and on the ground floor of the museum is a lapidarium with valuable examples of stone sculpture. The archaeological collection consists of items found during archaeological excavations in Budva (Hellenistic gold, various vases, jewelry, ornaments, tools, cutlery, glassware, clay, silverware...) dating from the 5th century BC to the Middle Ages, uniting the culture of the Illyrians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines and Slavs in this area. Especially valuable are gold earrings and a brooch showing an eagle with a boy in its claws, which is linked to the myth of Zeus and Ganymede. The ethnographic collection contains objects from this region from the 18th to the beginning of the 20th century. The archaeological collection has more than 1,200 items, and the ethnological collection has 450 items. Recently, the museum became home to an ancient mosaic, which is assumed to date from the 2nd century AD, which was accidentally found at the end of the 1930s, during the construction of the old hotel "Avala".

  • Ballerina statue

    • On the way to Mogren beach, which is located next to the old town, there is a statue of a ballerina, which is perhaps the most famous place for taking pictures in Budva. Local legend says that on the rock where this statue is today, a girl came every day and looked out to sea, waiting for a sailor. Her fiance sailed away and never came back. For years, the girl came to the rock and waited for her love. According to legend, one morning she was found dead on a rock. The statue is really beautiful and visitors will enjoy looking at it. It is certainly located on the way to the beach, and in addition to the fact that it is impossible not to notice it, it is also worth stopping and looking at this beautiful statue.

Traditions and Customs

  1. Budva

    • Celebration of St. St. John the Baptist

      • Celebration of St. St. John the Baptist (Budva Municipality Day) – St. John the Baptist is the patron saint of Budva, and on June 24 every year, this day is celebrated with various religious and cultural events. Many families from Budva go to the church of St. John in the old town, where the solemn liturgy is held.

    • Carnival and Masquerade Ball

      • Carnival and Masquerade Ball - During the spring, Budva organizes an international tourist carnival, an event that attracts many tourists. The carnival includes processions of masked groups, outdoor performances, as well as a masquerade ball, which is part of the old Budva tradition of celebrating spring.

    • Customs of fishermen

      • Customs of fishermen - As in other coastal cities, fishing was an important part of life in Budva. It is customary for fishermen, before going out to sea, to light a candle in the church of St. Nicholas, praying for his safe return. Also, sharing the catch with family and friends is a traditional sign of togetherness.

  2. Kotor

    • Bokele Navy

      • Bokel Navy - One of the most famous symbols of Kotor and its tradition is the Bokel Navy, the oldest maritime brotherhood on the Adriatic. Its roots date back to the 9th century. The Navy participates in numerous ceremonies and celebrations, the most famous of which is the procession on the feast of St. Tripun, the patron saint of the city of Kotor. The central event is the traditional dance of sailors with swords in front of the Cathedral of Saint Tripun.

    • Kotor Carnival

      • Kotor Carnival - Kotor has a long tradition of organizing carnivals, which are held twice a year - in winter and summer. The carnival spirit is deeply rooted in the city's culture, and includes processions of masked groups through the old town, concerts, dance performances and many other fun activities.

    • Camellia feast

      • Camellia Festival - In the town of Stoliv, near Kotor, the annual Camellia Festival is held in honor of this beautiful flower that was brought from faraway places. During the spring, flower exhibitions, cultural programs, and a contest for the most beautiful camellia are organized, which is a special part of the local tradition.

  3. Tivat

    • Boka night

      • Boka Night: This is one of the most famous events in Tivat, but also in the entire Boka Kotorska. It takes place in August and is a traditional maritime procession of decorated boats, accompanied by music and fireworks. The event celebrates the maritime tradition and culture of the region.

    • Fishing tradition

      • Fishing tradition: As in most coastal towns, fishing is deeply rooted in daily life in Tivat. There are a number of local events related to fishing, where residents gather for communal fish dinners, preparing local seafood specialties such as grilled fish, squid and other seafood.

  4. Perast

    • Mary's Assumption

      • Mary's Assumption: This event, celebrated on August 15, is one of the most important festivals in Perast. In honor of the Virgin, a boat procession is organized, where homage is paid to the church of Our Lady of Škrpjela, which is dedicated to the Virgin. This custom brings together locals and tourists, and boats are decorated with flowers.

    • Feast of Saint Nicholas

      • Feast of Saint Nicholas: Saint Nicholas is the patron saint of fishermen, and the feast in his honor is celebrated on December 6. Various events are organized on that day, including mass, festive meals and gatherings among fishermen and local residents.

Welcome to the landmarks of Bar

Want to know in-depth about town of Bar? Why not start with some of the towns most famous landmarks!

  • Old Olive

    • The old olive tree in Mirovica is one of the oldest olive trees in the world and is considered the oldest tree in Europe. Its age is estimated at over 2000 years, which is confirmed by a certificate from the University of Istanbul "Wood Anatomy and Tree-Ring Research Laboratory. It is located not far from the old town of Bar, along the main road that leads from Bar to Ulcinj. It is one of the most important assets of Montenegro. Due to its natural morphological, environmental, aesthetic and historical values, this exceptional natural monument was declared a natural asset and was placed under the protection of the state in 1957. It should be noted that even after 2000 years, this olive tree still bears fruit. Its former owners, the Nikočević family, botanists who came during the Italian occupation offered huge money to take it out and take it to Italy. They rejected it without thinking.

  • Legends of Old Olive

    • Legend has it that the entire area was named Mirovica because under an old olive tree, feuding armies, brotherhoods and families reconciled. Older Barans remember the ban on tearing branches or leaves from its tree, as well as the legend about the curse that would befall anyone who tries to destroy or cut it down. The legend says that in the past there was a rule that in the Bar area a man could not marry if he had not planted a well-known tree. the number of olive seedlings. A special cult has always been cultivated for the olive tree in Bar, because the "Old Lady" remembers much more than our history, remembers many wars, many deaths and memories, many agreements, reconciliations and healings. That is why it played a very important role in the material and spiritual life of the inhabitants of the Bar region. In the past, the owner did not sell the olive tree, and no one cut down the tree or stepped on the fruit.

  • Castle of King Nikola

    • The castle of King Nikola, the only Montenegrin king, is located on the promenade of King Nikola, near the city center. This palace complex was actually built by Petar Karađorđević, the Serbian king, who was the son-in-law of King Nikola. Ever since he married Zorka, the king's daughter, he lived in Montenegro. King Nicholas bought the castle from his son-in-law. The castle was built in 1885, on the seashore, and consists of a large castle, a small castle, a chapel, a guardhouse and a winter garden. In 1910, the castle got a ballroom. Next to the castle there is also a pier for ships and yachts, for friends who visited it, and at one time King Nikola himself had his own ten yachts. One of them named "Sybil" was bought from the novelist Jules Verne. There is also a flower shop in the courtyard as a gift from the Italian King Emmanuel, and today it serves as a restaurant called Knjaževa Bašta. King Nicholas married his second daughter to the Italian court. The real name of the castle is "Vila Topolica", which was given to it by Petar Karađorđević, after the town of Topola in Serbia, where Petar comes from. Inside the castle, it is possible to visit the rooms where King Nicholas stayed with his wife, children and friends.

  • Aqueduct

    • The Bar Aqueduct is a stone bridge, located north of Stari Bar, 4 km from the center of the city of Bar in Montenegro. The Bar Aqueduct is the only aqueduct in Montenegro, and one of the three largest and best preserved aqueducts in the former Yugoslavia, along with the Diocletian Aqueduct near Split in Croatia and the Skopje Aqueduct in North Macedonia. The Bar aqueduct was built like a bridge during the Ottoman rule, from the 16th to the 17th century, and served to deliver water from the spring on Mount Rumija, about 3 km away, to the old town of Bar. The bridge is supported by 17 stone arches, of different widths and heights, supported by 17 massive stone columns. Water stone pipes are laid at the highest level of the aqueduct, they have a diameter of 12 cm and are 30 cm long. The whole building was built from rough stone blocks. After the catastrophic earthquake that hit Montenegro on April 15, 1979 in the early hours of the morning (06:19), the aqueduct completely collapsed. After that, it was completely renovated, so that it still serves its basic purpose today.
